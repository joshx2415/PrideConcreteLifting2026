## Fort Worth Concrete Lifting — From the Stockyards to the Suburbs

Fort Worth is home base for us. We've been lifting concrete across this city for nearly three decades, and we know the ground under these neighborhoods as well as anyone.

The Tarrant County clay soil that Fort Worth sits on is some of the most reactive in the state. It swells when we get a good rain and shrinks back down in the summer heat. That constant push and pull is murder on concrete — driveways crack, sidewalks heave, pool decks settle, and garage aprons drop.

## Common Jobs We See in Fort Worth

* **Older neighborhoods** like Ridglea, Arlington Heights, and Westover Hills have homes with decades of soil movement under them. Driveways and walkways that were poured 30 or 40 years ago have had a lifetime of Texas weather working against them.
* **Newer developments** on the west and south sides of town often have settling issues because the fill dirt under new construction hasn't had time to fully compact.
* **Commercial properties** along I-30, I-35W, and the Camp Bowie corridor deal with parking lots and sidewalks that take heavy daily traffic on unpredictable soil.
